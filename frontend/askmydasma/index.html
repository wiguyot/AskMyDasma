Both look for points in sparsely populated regions, but calculate their scores differently:

  • PySAD adds up the population counts encountered along the entire path in each tree.
  • Paper uses only the population count of the region where traversal stops: a leaf or a region that is already sparse enough.

Another difference: PySAD starts from the same bounds for all trees; Paper varies these bounds randomly from one tree to another.

Paper implements the original article’s per-tree bounds and terminal-node scoring, using PySAD as its foundation. This does not necessarily mean better performance on your data.
